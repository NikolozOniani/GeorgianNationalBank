# NBG policy data, 2020–2024: validation and IMF benchmark

## What was actually acquired

The [monthly panel](nbg_policy_monthly_2020_2024.csv) contains 60 calendar months. Policy rate in force at month end, reserve stocks/components and changes, an official USD/GEL quote requested for each calendar month end (with actual effective date retained), national month-over-month CPI, and monthly mean TIBR overnight rates each have 60 observations. [Policy decisions](nbg_policy_decisions_2008_onward.csv) contain 173 dated decisions since 2008, including **40 in 2020–2024**. [Daily TIBR](nbg_tibr_daily_2020_2024.csv) has **1,250 business-day observations**; the source decimal rate is multiplied by 100 to express percent. [Quarterly external controls](nbg_external_quarterly_2020_2024.csv) contain 20 current-account and reserve-asset flow observations, never interpolated into months. The source-by-field [dictionary](nbg_series_dictionary.csv) and [manifest](nbg_source_manifest.json) record source URLs, SHA-256, units, frequency, missingness and disclosure caveats. Raw source files are re-fetched into ignored `data/raw/`, with hashes pinned to the acquired vintage.

The monthly intervention purchase/sale, government FX transaction, reserve FX/gold valuation, and other-movement fields are **blank for all 60 months**. These blanks are not zero. The verified [8 October 2024 auction](nbg_auction_events_verified.csv) is one executed, publicly disclosed sale of **USD 48.5 million** at a weighted average **2.7194 GEL/USD**. Its announcement date, actual auction date and result agree in the NBG release; no offer/announcement amount prior to execution was inferred. The [2024 annual benchmark](nbg_fx_annual_benchmarks.csv) separately records NBG net sales through seven auctions of **USD 382.1 million**, BMatch net sales **USD 52.65 million**, and combined net sales **USD 434.75 million**. The one extracted dated auction does not establish monthly totals or the other six auction dates. BMatch is a monthly disclosed aggregate, not an event dated to the disclosure day.

## Accounting checks and limits

The six reserve components in the NBG SDMX feed sum to the headline stock in every one of the 60 months (maximum absolute floating-point discrepancy below `1e-9` USD million). The December 2023–December 2024 stock change is **−USD 561.0498 million**, agreeing to rounding with the annual report's **−USD 561.1 million**. The 2024 net sales sum `382.1 + 52.65 = 434.75` USD million. The difference between the stock change and net intervention is **−USD 126.30 million**, but this is an **unreconciled composite** of government FX flows, valuation, investment income, and other transactions. It is neither an estimate of intervention nor a single identified other-flow category. The NBG report mentions government debt service and external financing; no monthly transaction decomposition or consistent timing was obtained. Auction settlement is next business day, while reserve stock is month end. We do not force the two onto a false same-day ledger.

## Measurement and availability

The NBG policy-rate workbook labels decision and enforcement dates separately. Monthly policy rate is the last rate effective by month end, and the separate event file preserves the decision date. Official reserves are end-month USD million stocks, not USD intervention flows. The exchange-rate API returns a quoted rate and `validFromDate`; the panel records both the requested calendar month end and actual effective date, as a weekend date can return a prior valid rate. NBG describes the fix as calculated from BMatch trades in the prior-day to current-day 16:30 window, announced by 17:00, and effective the next day. Thus the official fix is not an intraday price immediately before an auction. TIBR is a business-day interbank rate, with its 2018 reform predating the panel; its monthly mean is descriptive, not an operation cost. The CPI series was acquired from Geostat and is a contemporaneous outcome, not an exogenous control.

The NBG publishes auction results shortly after execution but BMatch intervention statistics around **25 days after month end**. The source workbooks and SDMX files lack observation-specific historical publication timestamps or complete revision histories. The current download is a 2026 vintage; no real-time 2020–2024 information set can be reconstructed from it alone. Deposit/lending rates, NEER/REER, sterilization quantities/costs, government FX flows and complete FX transaction files remain unresolved; no series was invented to fill them. The first verified BMatch mechanism was introduced in 2020. The NSDP `exd-geo.xml` file was inspected and contains a *quarterly* `D_USD` indicator with ambiguous scale, so it was excluded from the monthly USD/GEL panel. The monthly official API is used instead.

## IMF 2025 benchmark and falsifiability

The IMF [2025 Selected Issues report](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2025/english/1geoea2025002-source-pdf.pdf), chapter pp. 4–18 (download SHA-256 `696a28892665a1c94a462fcf62b1ad733c9739c8998bd62622ef6a4a3c9d487b`), evaluates 2020–2024 interventions with an estimated Bayesian QIPF model. Its FXI input is a proxy based mainly on Adler et al. and additional NBG information, **not a complete verified transaction file**. Its no-intervention counterfactual peaks at core inflation **11%** and the policy rate **13.4%**, **1.5** and **2.4 percentage points** higher than realized. Those are model-dependent counterfactuals, not causal estimates from an external instrument. The report says the 2024 election and pandemic contained fundamental news as well as sentiment; its model lacks explicit news shocks and may mistake one for the other. It also flags reserve depletion, communication and FX-market-development costs. It does not measure heterogeneous household price baskets, currency debts/assets, employment, remittance revaluation, or crisis-insurance benefits in one household welfare ledger. Its short-run macro benefit need not imply a particular distributional or long-run welfare sign.

The IMF describes around **USD 700 million sales across September–October 2024** in its intervention discussion, whereas NBG's *full-year net* 2024 auction-plus-BMatch figure is **USD 434.75 million**. These are different windows and likely gross/net concepts; the current transaction archive cannot reconcile them. Calling the difference an error or using either number as a monthly flow would be unjustified.

Candidate tests require data beyond this panel: an auction event study requires all executed auction dates/volumes, announcement and execution times, intraday pre/post quotes, expectations and concurrent news; BMatch analysis requires the monthly executed flows and a separately justified surprise/assignment rule, since its later disclosure date is not the execution date; an IV/RD design requires a documented exogenous trigger, manipulation tests and exclusion, none identified. A structural replication requires QIPF code, real-time input vintages and transparent restrictions; sensitivity should add news shocks, separate purchases and sales, and vary reserve-insurance costs. Prospective household analysis can join identified price responses to the validated 2017 basket only after an exogenous policy innovation and price channel are established. **GO** for accounting and descriptive timelines; **NO-GO** for causal policy effects or net household welfare claims.
