# Research audit and publication-readiness assessment

*The Economics of Reserve Accumulation in Georgia: An Independent Assessment of Costs, Benefits, and Optimal Reserve Adequacy* · Study author Nikoloz Oniani

**Version reviewed** [3033989257582a7a2a226a2e3e8569f964eb1017](https://github.com/NikolozOniani/GeorgianNationalBank/tree/3033989257582a7a2a226a2e3e8569f964eb1017) · **25 November 2026**

**Fixed question** — Did the National Bank of Georgia's large-scale foreign-currency purchases prevent the Georgian lari from appreciating, and were the resulting economic costs justified by the benefits of reserve accumulation?

**Overall research grade: 64/100. Readiness to draft a conditional applied-economics paper: 82/100. Readiness to submit the current project to a journal: 44/100.** The study can already sustain a paper about what competing purchase policies would exchange in currency strength, financing expense and liquidity protection. Its strongest original evidence locates the point at which additional dollars stop covering a specified Georgian funding stress. Its weakest link is the translation of those dollars into national welfare. No estimated Georgian relationship ranks the reserve stocks by expected welfare.

## Answers to the fixed question

**Did the purchases prevent the lari from appreciating?** Across the registered window, the lari *did* appreciate. NBG bought USD 5,803.15m net and one lari bought 7.74% more dollars on 30 September 2026 than on 31 December 2024 (2.8068 to 2.6051 GEL/USD). The economically important question is whether purchases prevented **further** appreciation. The central model says yes and gives 2.3558 without purchases, or 10.58% more USD per lari than observed at the endpoint. The conservative and aggressive cases give 2.69% and 26.97%. All three assume that purchases weaken the currency on impact. The historical Georgian observations do not independently select their sign, magnitude or duration. Zero impact reproduces 2.6051. The opposite-sign diagnostic gives 2.8808. The strongest defensible phrase is “restrained appreciation under the stated calibrations.” [R1 transactions](../r1_2026_11_10/episode_totals.csv) · [R3 historical evidence](../r3_fx_response/README.md) · [R7 challenges](../r7_synthesis/fx_reversals.csv)

**Were the resulting economic costs justified by reserve benefits?** Some marginal dollars buy a clearly specified settlement service. Under the September at-par assumption, an additional USD 188.60m above the no-purchases portfolio covers the twelve-month scheduled public gap. Repeating the pandemic market-sales policy at episode import scale raises that amount to USD 2,631.83m. Those are amounts of *cash available to meet calls*. The same USD 100 can retire a USD 100 debt without generating USD 100 of additional national income. At half policy-rate funding, the episode net interest expense is GEL 28.68m; its sign changes at a 46.39% remunerated share. The actual marginal funding mix is unknown. A separate five-year national-resource test requires a 2.55 percentage-point reduction in annual first-crisis risk if the alternative net external return is 1% higher, the first loss is USD 3.81bn, the starting annual risk is 10% and real discounting is 3%. At a one-point cut the conditional result is roughly GEL 400m negative; at three points it is GEL 121m positive, in December 2024 purchasing power. Available Georgian evidence establishes neither the actual cut nor the attainable net return. ## Two reviewer challenges the published headline needs

**1. Hold gross reserves at USD 5bn and change only the timing rule.** For each scenario, keep monthly purchases between zero and the R1 minimum-gross amount actually available for that month, preserve common sales, apply the same SOFR accrual and solve the existing central R3 FX and monetary equations. Fill the required terminal lot with the earliest or latest eligible buys, allowing a fractional last fill. Each policy finishes with exactly USD 5bn gross and USD 3.476bn liquid at par. These are retrospective sensitivity cases with the same month-end news convention. Policymakers did not have the September terminal budget and future purchase opportunities in hand at the start.

| Purchases retained | Gross purchases, USDbn | Sept. GEL/USD | Extra USD per GEL vs observed | Episode net interest cost, GELm at half remuneration | Month-ends with a twelve-month scaled-sales shortfall, out of 21 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Earliest available | 2.681 | **2.4287** | 7.26% | 24.41 | **4** |
| Same percentage each month, published policy | 2.726 | **2.4694** | 5.49% | 14.01 | **12** |
| Latest available | 2.769 | **2.5108** | 3.75% | 5.46 | **18** |

Earlier retained dollars earn interest for longer and protect earlier openings. Earlier purchases also have more time for their calibrated FX effect to decay. At the same terminal stock, the earliest schedule provides the most protection at earlier origins and incurs the largest cumulative interest cost among the three. All three portfolios can settle their assumed purchase paths; their shortfalls above refer to subsequent hypothetical stresses. These three examples disprove a unique mapping from September reserve stock to the lari or historical protection. The two extreme purchase schedules do not establish statistical or general-equilibrium bounds. The published proportional calculation reproduces. [Reviewer results](checks.json) · [Executable calculation](../../replication/audit_study_2026_11_25.py)

**2. Combine a securities haircut with the eligible bank call.** At par the September scaled-sales requirement is USD 3,316.61m usable. The corresponding gross threshold, USD 4,841.02m, is transparent arithmetic: USD 8,166.92m observed gross less USD 6,642.51m narrow FX at par, plus USD 3,316.61m required. The threshold therefore does not depend on the transferred FX elasticity within this stock accounting. At USD 5bn gross, the R7 5% haircut on securities leaves USD 3,336.90m usable and only USD 20.29m headroom. If the separate solvent, collateralized, **disjoint** USD 100m bank call also occurs, the portfolio falls **USD 79.71m short**. Within the same R7 asset-mix convention, the joint threshold rises to **USD 5.083bn**. The original frontier correctly labels its haircut and bank-call checks as separate. The joint case shows why the distinction belongs alongside the headline. Neither the bank call nor a joint-event probability is observed. ## Data value, assumptions and originality

**Keep in the argument.** The monthly transaction ledger, official quote, signed reserve bridge, asset composition, maturity buckets, audited interest, actual CD operations and a few observed historical shocks directly change the answer. Keep the unsuccessful auction and holdout tests because they test rival explanations. The 2025 IMF Integrated Policy Framework exercise supplies a serious competing mechanism: intervention can cushion inefficient risk-premium shocks in a shallow market. Its modeled short-run gains for 2020–24 cannot settle the later accumulation episode. Cangul and Abel's separate 2026 exercise values crisis consumption, sovereign spreads, dollarization and market liquidity; its 145–150% of ARA at 10% crisis probability answers a broader question than the cash-cover calculation. [2025 IMF assessment](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2025/english/1geoea2025002-source-pdf.pdf) · [2026 IMF benchmark](https://doi.org/10.5089/9798229051323.018.A001)

**Keep available, move out of the main argument.** The 40,338-row country transfer extract can check inflow composition but supplies no exogenous purchase surprise. Daily exchange-rate observations do not turn monthly BMatch totals into dated interventions. The long depository survey, detailed CPI diagnostics and Treasury/security records earn space when they bound a model input, identify a feasible funding alternative, or change an incidence result. Preserve microdata extracts, earlier credit examples, acquisition inventories and historical editorial material in their designated supporting roles. Deleting them would sacrifice auditability without strengthening identification. [Data catalogue](../../data/derived/episode_2026_11_09/catalogue.json) · [Evidence catalogue](../../evidence/README.md)

**Original mathematics and scenarios.** The independent contribution is stronger than a compilation: matching purchase lots to funding flows, valuing the same stock against peak cumulative calls, testing which marginal tranche ceases to provide coverage, reconstructing ARA vintages and applying derivative/domain checks to the IMF model. Household accounting provides a smaller incidence contribution. Exponential decay, rate parity, discounted first-event losses, bond pricing and the positive-part shortfall formula are established tools. Novelty lies in their disciplined application and counterexamples. More rows on the same sensitivity grid add little. A claim to a new general theory or a globally novel method would need a separate literature comparison.

**What prevents a welfare answer.** A resident selling USD to NBG may exchange one asset for another. The country does not mechanically forgo a real investment equal to every dollar the central bank acquires. The paper must specify who would otherwise hold the foreign asset, what return or debt payment is feasible, and what national risk changes. Domestic interest transfers, GEL valuation marks, imported-goods price changes and public FX principal cannot all be added to the opportunity-cost hurdle. Similarly, the September template reports USD 1.817bn in section III.1 contingent liabilities in addition to II.1 scheduled drains. Its call probability, beneficiary eligibility, timing and overlap with bank FX liquidity are unresolved. A zero unconditional call and an automatic full call would both be unsupported. [Matched funding accounts](../r7_synthesis/METHODS.md) · [Dated schedules](../reserve_buffer_policy/scheduled_calls.csv)

For policies A and B, R7 solves for a **required** reduction in expected losses equal to the opportunity-cost difference. To rank them empirically one needs the **achieved or credibly bounded** difference. An interior welfare optimum additionally requires marginal expected benefit and marginal national resource cost as functions of holdings, evaluated at consistent timing, and comparison with feasible boundaries. A dated cash-sufficiency threshold contributes evidence on one service. It cannot identify that optimum. The published 1.39-point hurdle for USD 5bn versus USD 8.167bn, for example, assumes annual first-crisis risk of 10% **under the USD 5bn policy**, a USD 3.81bn loss and a 1% annual alternative premium. That 10% is an input to the comparison, not a Georgian hazard estimate. [Interval insurance hurdles](../r7_frontier/insurance_hurdles.csv)

**Decision for peer review.** Send the R1 accounts, historical falsification results, R7 frontier and these challenges for **technical review now**. A manuscript that asserts actual prevented appreciation, national impoverishment or a welfare-optimal stock would fail on inference. A conditional country study can proceed while the upper-level welfare question remains open.
