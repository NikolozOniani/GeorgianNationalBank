# External reviewer brief

**Requested review:** empirical accuracy, contractual arithmetic, interpretation, and whether the stated accountability conclusions follow. The political title is a question under investigation. Reject any wording that converts a conditional burden into an identified net policy loss.

## Read in this order

1. Manuscript abstract, Sections 3–5 and the three figures.
2. `series_dictionary.csv`, `Sources.md`, and a sample of `credit_observations.csv` against the official workbooks.
3. `claim_ledger.csv` and the reproduction commands in the packet README.
4. The prior accountability dossier only for the broader investigation; the credit calculations are self-contained.

## Principal objections and the paper's responses

| Objection | Response in this version | What could still overturn or narrow the result |
|---|---|---|
| The title presupposes the answer. | The abstract, introduction and conclusion expressly withhold a net impoverishment finding. The scientific subtitle states the narrower estimand. | Any public summary that drops those qualifications should be rewritten. |
| Retail averages do not identify policy pass-through. | No such coefficient is estimated. Housing and consumer rates are shown together, including opposite movements. | Loan-level controls could change the interpretation of the aggregate comparison. |
| A 20-year GEL 100,000 loan is not representative. | Both dimensions are assumptions. The grid varies size and term; no population scaling is made. | Real balance/maturity distributions could make aggregate incidence substantially different. |
| Keeping the policy rate at 8% is an implausible alternative. | It is an accounting reference only. A 9% reference and reset sensitivity are reported. | An identified macro alternative could change the welfare sign, even with positive contractual costs. |
| The simulation applies a contract that has not been observed. | It is explicitly synthetic. The three-point margin and reset rules are not measured loan terms. | Actual caps, floors, reset dates, subsidies, refinancing or payment deferrals change the path. |
| Higher payments omit principal accumulation. | Payment, interest and terminal debt differences reconcile; each is reported separately. | A different amortization or capitalization convention requires re-estimation. |
| Borrowers also gained from income growth and stabilization. | Real-wage improvement, easing and IMF model-based intervention benefits are retained as counterevidence. No net welfare total is claimed. | Linked income/assets and a credible policy alternative are required to quantify offsets. |
| Household floating-rate shares could scale the example. | Available shares use incompatible populations or lack currency/reset cross-tabs; multiplication is explicitly rejected. | A correctly defined joint distribution would permit exposure weighting, with additional assumptions. |
| There are no confidence intervals. | Deterministic conditional arithmetic has no sampling error. Revision, aggregation and contract uncertainty are documented; sensitivity ranges are not confidence intervals. | A sampled borrower study would need survey/design or sampling-based uncertainty. |
| A disclosure gap is being presented as concealment. | The claim is limited to records assembled in this study. No bad faith or legal breach is alleged. | Locating an already-public cross-tab would close that gap and should change the wording. |

## Specific checks requested

- Confirm that LMR housing/consumer rows and L3.8.1 household columns use the populations described. Check whether flows include renegotiation or refinancing in ways that should be more explicit.
- Verify the stated end-month FX conversion, zero-rate exclusions and stock/flow weighting from the source notes. Do not accept a stock multiplied by a rate as a national interest bill without matching denominators.
- Audit at least the December 2020/2022/2024 headline cells. Check the sector/residency/purpose identities independently.
- Verify policy decision versus effective dates and the convention of a reset at the start of a month. Evaluate whether the chosen full-reset phase grid is adequate as a conditional exercise.
- Check the annuity formula and the terminal balance identity independently, rather than only rerunning the same code.
- Assess whether the 8% and 9% reference paths and the omitted fees, default and refinancing channels are described prominently enough.
- Identify any source revision, definition change or publicly available contract distribution missing from this packet. Supply its precise URL or record reference.
- Assess whether the proposed aggregate disclosure is proportionate, legally permissible and sufficiently protected against borrower re-identification.

## Decision requested from reviewers

Classify each material claim as **supported as written**, **supported after qualification**, **requires new data**, or **incorrect**. For an objection, identify the source cell, formula, assumption or sentence at issue and the correction that would resolve it. The repository's claim ledger supports this process.

This is suitable for external review as a descriptive-data and conditional-contract paper. It is not ready to be presented as proof that NBG caused a quantified national welfare or poverty loss. That stronger claim would require evidence absent here.
