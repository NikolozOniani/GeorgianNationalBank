# Public FX payments during Georgia’s 2009 and 2020 funding shocks

19 November 2026. Two historical stress windows from [roadmap section 7](../../../research/ROADMAP.md). All dollar figures are USD. A public borrower can have ample financing over a quarter and still need cash before its lenders disburse. The dates establish that ordering in Georgia. They identify the size of particular payment obligations more closely than the national reserve requirement.

## 2009Q1 | Four IMF repayments preceded the March drawing

- Georgia paid SDR 7.38m of IMF principal in four instalments between 22 January and 20 March. Each amount and date matches the schedule dated 31 December 2008. Valued at each payment day's IMF rate, the four payments total **$11.03m**. The schedule also lists SDR 860,550 in charges on Sunday 1 February. Including those charges on the nominal date raises the pre-drawing total to **$12.32m**. Their cash settlement date and final amount remain unverified.
- The IMF released SDR 126.2m on **25 March**, after all four principal payments. SDR 63.1m had already become available at the December review and remained undrawn. A second SDR 63.1m became available at the March review. If the first tranche could have been drawn in time and used for those payments, it alone covered the entire SDR 8.24m scheduled subset. With zero opening cash and no earlier draw, that same subset required $12.32m. These two timing cases describe specified IMF obligations. The member transaction table does not identify the domestic paying account for each instalment. The BOP allocates the quarterly net IMF credit to NBG.
- The general government separately recorded **GEL 13m of external amortization, GEL 13m of interest to nonresidents and GEL 37m of gross external borrowing**. The latter includes zero IMF budget borrowing. For these two fiscal payment lines alone, zero opening cash gives a timing envelope from zero to roughly GEL 26m: the low end requires timely usable borrowing, the high end places both payments first. Original currency constraints could prevent pooling. Government used GEL 40m of sovereign wealth fund resources and GEL 72m of deposits at NBG and banks. These observations establish fiscal flows in the reporting currency. They leave their original currencies, payment dates and the Treasury's free opening FX balance unresolved. Assigning the March central-bank drawing to the fiscal budget would count the wrong recipient.
- Bank of Georgia reports that it fully repaid a **$65m** Merrill Lynch senior facility in January. The prospectus describes a 13-month loan obtained in December 2008. January is the reported repayment month, so this evidence cannot establish a January contractual maturity or forced rollover failure. The bank was the debtor. This face amount cannot be added to the banking sector’s net loan repayment, its other cumulative wholesale redemptions, or public obligations. [Prospectus, physical p111, and IMF review](../cases/pdf_sources.json)
- Gross official reserves opened at **$1,480.16m** and fell **$210.83m** by end-February before recovering in March. The IMF technical memorandum gives opening program net reserves of $879.8m. Table 5 of the same review prints $909m for December 2008 at program rates. We retain the $29.2m discrepancy. End-March actual net reserves of $777m exceeded the adjusted program floor of $677m by $100m. This is a quarter-end performance test. It does not establish a daily operating floor or available Treasury cash. [Quarterly fiscal and program observations](quarter_inputs.csv)

Its separate debtor breakdown gives $103.6m government and $0.8m NBG, totaling $104.4m with independent rounding. The government amount includes about $0.07m guaranteed debt service. The bulletin does not establish a new guarantee call. The schedule exceeds the principal-plus-interest outturn by **$10.47m**. The agency perimeter, instrument types, conversion dates, and schedule versus realization differ. No loan-level crosswalk allocates the difference to arrears, cancellations or valuation. Adding the two accounts would duplicate payments. - A $500m government bond outstanding at 31 March carried a 6.875% annual coupon payable semiannually. Its nominal 12 April coupon was **$17.1875m**. The date fell on a Sunday. The bond terms allow a business-day delay. MoF records $17.2m of Q2 bond interest, consistent within rounding. The cash value date is missing. The coupon is already included in the quarter's interest. - The 31 March IMF expectations schedule listed SDR 592,870 of assessments and charges on 30 April and 1 May. Together with the coupon these give **$18.00m** of scheduled uses through 1 May. This calculation assigns each projected charge to its nominal date and assumes the coupon settles by 1 May. The 2020 obligations-basis query returned no observations. The expectations schedule supplies projected charges, and the dated principal ledger contains no entry that confirms their settlement. - The government IMF budget-support drawing occurred on **5 May**, four days after approval. The SDR 147m drawing values at $200.34m using the IMF daily rate. MoF records $201.3m of gross IMF budget disbursements and the BOP records $200.67m net government IMF credit. These presentations use different valuation or netting conventions. The approximately $0.96m MoF-to-daily-valuation difference remains unallocated. - Government external drawings totaled **$468.1m** in Q2. Gross drawings exceeded its reported $103.6m service by about $364.5m. Project-restricted receipts cannot automatically fund Treasury debt service. If the only cash uses are MoF's reported external public debt service, the zero-opening peak uncovered payment lies between zero and approximately $104.3m, allowing for source rounding. Paying all bills before any usable receipts yields the gross-service ceiling. The lower endpoint requires other usable receipts to arrive in time for each debtor. The quarter totals cannot verify either ordering. This envelope excludes intervention, bank support, and any unreported obligations.

## What follows for reserve adequacy

The [restricted peak calculation](restricted_peaks.csv) starts at zero, sums payments less receipts by day, and takes the largest positive cumulative value. It combines the named IMF and bond obligations only for the labeled scenario, assuming dollar-equivalent amounts can be pooled and converted at the stated valuations. Fees, spreads and currency-specific settlement constraints are outside the calculation. Its two 2009 values ($11.03m actual principal and $12.32m including projected charges) and its 2020 scheduled value ($18.00m) answer how much the *listed* payments would have needed before the listed funding. Missing receipts can lower a real gap and missing obligations can raise it. The zero in the 2020 dated-IMF-only row means the extract contains a drawing and no dated principal repayment.

Across all reserve accounts, 2009Q1 had $142m net market sales and a $12.95m stock increase. In 2020Q2 the corresponding figures were $120.95m and $215.57m. After accounting for sales, other net reserve transactions were $201.80m and $310.63m respectively. Valuation, other stock changes and vintage differences jointly contributed −$46.85m and +$25.89m. These are residuals. Government borrowing enters reserves only to the extent proceeds reach and remain in the reserve account. [Reserve stock–flow bridge](reserve_bridge.csv) · [Underlying BOP, stock and intervention series](../cases/README.md)

**Decision.** These episodes support proceeding to the policy definitions in roadmap step 2. Neither establishes the largest unavoidable draw on NBG reserves. A positive lower bound on that draw requires dated public FX obligations exceeding accessible Treasury FX balances and receipts at the relevant settlement dates. The critical missing records are the opening Treasury balance by currency and restriction, gross service value dates by debtor, and value dates and use restrictions on the remaining external disbursements. Support for banks and discretionary market sales require separate policy scenarios. A reserve optimum or crisis probability cannot be inferred from either historical peak.

## Replication and source boundaries

From the repository root run `python src/historical_payment_calendar.py --check` for an offline rebuild and `python -m unittest discover -s tests -p test_historical_payment_calendar.py` for independent arithmetic and classification checks. With the locked sources cached, add `--from-raw` to verify the original IMF schedules, exchange rates, member cash transactions, and NBG template. Add `--acquire` to retrieve missing locked files. Dependencies are Python, lxml and openpyxl. A provider revision fails the hash check. IMF daily USD-per-SDR valuations use the last available quote on or before the event. For the Sunday 2009 charge this is 30 January. USD equivalents indicate valuation, and original currency and observation status are retained.

[Raw input hashes and URLs](source_locks.json) cover the downloaded sources. [IMF actual transactions](../cases/imf_sources.json) have a separate lock. [Quarter observations](quarter_inputs.csv) and [financing milestones](financing.csv) are reviewed transcriptions with source locators. The bond offering circular, World Bank and ADB document links above were independently read online and have no locked local original in this release. The calendar covers two selected quarters. It records retrospective outcomes separately from dated forecasts. Some historical first-publication dates remain unavailable.
