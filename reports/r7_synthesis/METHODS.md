## Marginal dollars and financing

Let $P_t$ be minimum purchases removed in USD, $e_t$ the observed month-end GEL/USD, $u_t$ the observed annual SOFR in percent and $i_t$ the annual policy rate in percent. A purchase joins the USD and GEL ledgers at month-end. For each month the following balances start from zero at the common opening,

$$
A_t=(1+u_t/1200)A_{t-1}+P_t,\qquad K_t=K_{t-1}+e_t P_t.
$$

$A_t$ is the USD lot difference implied by R3. It must separately reconcile to both the gross reserve gap and the narrow FX asset gap. $K_t$ tracks the GEL principal of its assumed counterpart. Settlement-day and execution quotes are unavailable. Missing USD interest is a proxy for the yield on the marginal assets. Interest accrues from the following month. A constant monthly paired amount on unknown gross legs adds one USD principal unit per affected month, compounded at the same rates. Dividing remaining alternative liquid FX by that cumulative unit balance gives the month-specific settlement threshold.

Interest on the marginal amount is $I_t=e_t A_{t-1}u_t/1200$. For a funding share $f$, the GEL cost is $F_t=fK_{t-1}i_t/1200$. Use the observed policy rate for remunerated funding on the purchases side, or the alternative policy rate for foregone refinancing income on the no-purchases side. Each is a separate mechanism. The remaining share has zero marginal interest. A 100% rate-equivalent counterpart is a price and quantity stress. The history of listed CDs rules out interpreting it as all actual CD funding. Loan demand, collateral, instrument-level balances, interest on common positions, hedging, fees and duration of incremental assets remain open. If GEL 1bn of an additional common liability repriced one-for-one with a 1 pp annual rate differential for a full year, it would add GEL 10m on that separate exposure.

Interest-only cost is $\sum(F_t-I_t)$. The mark on the USD lot less historical GEL principal is $M=e_T A_T-K_T$. It equals interest received plus the sum of prior USD balances times monthly quote changes. The marked window cost is $\sum F_t-M$. A hypothetical liquidation at $e_T=(K_T+\sum F_t)/A_T$ breaks even in nominal GEL. For present values, discount actual month-end funding streams separately with $(1+d)^{-t/12}/D_t$, where $D_t$ is observed headline CPI relative to December 2024. Discount the retained asset and principal once at the cutoff. The two cost conventions must never be added. Aggregate NBG net income, realized reserve interest and the marked gain on gold belong to other accounting perimeters.

## Stress, access and marginal protection

For each December 2024–September 2026 origin construct the projected cash uses $U_j$ by months after origin. Section II.1 supplies signed cumulative net drains at 1, 3 and 12 months. Difference the buckets. Apply the incremental public uses at month 1, 3 and 12 in the reference and at 1, 2 and 4 in the timing sensitivity. These are timing cases within the reported buckets. The counterfactual retains the public schedule as a common shock. A Treasury payment funded from an account at NBG can still require reserve delivery. Only a matched external funding receipt arriving by the due date can offset the corresponding use. The 50% external-offset alternative assumes such coverage for half of positive net public uses. No unconditional credit line is verified.

The market budget uses the twelve separate monthly net sales from April 2020 through March 2021, totaling USD 980.472m. The scale equals the two preceding months' mean merchandise imports before each opening divided by January–February 2020 mean imports. For September 2026 those means are USD 1,681.79m and 674.70m, giving **2.4919**. Imports proxy for the size of the FX market exposed to a trade shock. They do not estimate private currency demand or required intervention. The origin month itself is excluded. The data vintage is retrospective and the short historical denominator can be seasonally unrepresentative. The nominal replay tests that choice. The separate 2008, 2009 and 2020–24 peak-window comparators retain their own horizons and report net need at that horizon. A quarterly total does not supply a first payment date. The illustrative additional USD 100m bank call occurs in month 1 and requires prior deduction of own resources, eligible collateral, solvency and disjoint uses. The underlying bank-specific amount is missing.

Let $B$ be stock accessible at the opening and $F$ a common dollar floor. Required opening liquidity is $Q=F+\max(0,\max_{k\le H}\sum_{j=1}^{k}U_j)$. At each projected group of obligations the first breach occurs when cumulative uses plus the floor exceed $B$. The marginal service of a tranche that raises usable stock from $B$ to $B+a$ is $[Q-B]_+-[Q-B-a]_+$. A breach of a positive floor can precede a failure to pay. Each origin includes the first cut from observed resources that would breach its floor at USD 100m resolution, allowing a smaller last lot. The September table also adds consecutive tranches starting from the no-purchases position. The formula reproduces any intermediate dollar amount at any origin. Smallest opening needs are in usable currency, conditional on the specified policy objective.

Primary usable stock equals deposits plus securities at reported values and signed negative other positions. Both economies assume cross-currency settlement at those values. They exclude gold, SDR and IMF positions. For a haircut or deposit-only case, allocate the missing lot across deposits and securities in their contemporaneous observed proportions, leaving their combined alternative balance consistent with R3. That split is unknown. The gold-access case allows immediate delivery of the full reported gold value in both worlds. Missing encumbrances and gross intrabucket settlements can raise actual needs. The 1% operating floor uses the same dollar amount in both economies. The post-cutoff stress experiments hold inherited portfolios available for the opening cash test. They do not forecast subsequent acquisitions, the path of the lari in a crisis or the effect of actually carrying out new emergency sales.

The marginal holding-cost test holds the September rate and SOFR fixed, scales simple annual carry by $H/12$, and compares it to the same horizon's protection. For the tranche table a constant 10% annual event risk implies an event probability $1-0.9^{H/12}$. Blank loss-per-dollar results mean no cash protection to price at that tranche. If the required probability of the stated event exceeds one, the hypothesized replacement cost cannot suffice. Financing, contingencies and bank backstops are never stacked with overlapping template claims.

## Comparable horizons for economic value

**Financial account.** At September 2026 hypothetically settle the mark against the historical GEL principal, then refinance the inherited incremental asset at its market value. This separates the earlier mark from subsequent annual interest. Future purchases and sales are common between worlds for this valuation. Hold the resulting USD difference fixed and distribute future earnings. Conditional continuation assumptions are a flat September price level and exchange rate, unchanged rates, access to rollover and equal terminal asset and funding principal. Under these assumptions the two principals offset on liquidation at the chosen terminal date. This last-step convention closes the financial account. Remaining insurance value, externalities and welfare effects after liquidation are unknown.

Write $a$ for September's USD lot and $r$ and $u$ for annual funding and USD rates in fractions. Prospective cost is $a(fr-u)\sum_{j=1}^n(1+d)^{-j}$. Convert September USD to December 2024 real GEL PV at $e_T/[D_T(1+d)^{21/12}]$. Add the marked historical PV only once. Use $n=5$ through September 2031, with alternatives of 1, 3 and 10 years. Test $d=0,3,5\%$, and future USD yields one pp either side of the September anchor. The R6 financial-hurdle illustration takes $\alpha\max(C,0)$ with $\alpha=0,0.5,1$. This is a cost-equivalent sensitivity. It neither identifies a resource cost nor credits a book gain as a national benefit.

**Resource account.** An independently posed hurdle is the net external resource return $z$ forgone annually per dollar held. This is an effective spread after reserve earnings, relative risk, duration, displacement fraction and any recoverable transfers. A real opportunity would have to be identified and deliverable at this scale to assign a value to $z$. Through the window a foregone dollar of net resources would accrue in the no-purchases economy. Convert it at that world's exchange rate and price level. Set $v_t=e^N_t/[D_t p_t(1+d)^{t/12}]$, with $p_t$ the ratio of alternative to observed headline price levels. Discounted exposure in December 2024 real GEL-years is

$$
X=\sum_{t=1}^{21}v_t A_{t-1}/12+v_T A_T\sum_{j=1}^{n}(1+d)^{-j}.
$$

Thus resource cost is $zX$. The prospective resource screen uses the frozen September alternative real conversion for all incremental losses and opportunity costs in the same numeraire. It assumes no additional pre-cutoff crisis gain, no terminal residual resource gain and identical subsequent net market purchases. The model treats reserve exposure as maintained over the horizon in all states. The stipulated first-crisis severity must include any net depletion, replenishment and later crisis consequences for that stress. Do not also charge the amount delivered against the same loss.

For a first crisis at each year-end with constant annual conditional probability $p$ and loss $L$, the September loss PV is $J(p)=L\sum_{j=1}^n p(1-p)^{j-1}/(1+d)^j$. The probability of any crisis by year $n$ is $1-(1-p)^n$. The model counts the first loss once. $L$ is 5%, 10% or 20% of the R6 USD 38.1bn benchmark. This uses a fixed dollar scale equal to rounded 2025 GDP, in September-equivalent purchasing units. It does not forecast future GDP. The maximum justifiable opportunity spread is $v_T[J(p_0)-J(p_1)]/X$. Solve this relation for $p_0-p_1$ at a chosen $z$. An empty hazard threshold means even reducing the assumed hazard to zero would not meet the hurdle. A zero threshold means that particular hurdle is already covered. A terminal value or another disjoint benefit enters linearly in the same PV units. The resulting surplus or deficit ranks only these proposed resource channels. Risk tolerance, distributions of future losses and bank-specific damages remain unknown.

**Sovereign issue.** With observed and alternative December 2025 gross reserves $R_O,R_N$, add $\theta\,0.0575\log[(38100+R_O)/(38100+R_N)]$ to the observed yield to price the no-purchases bond. Freeze the December gap through January pricing because intramonth execution is unavailable. Apply $\theta=0,0.5,1$. Discount the same face and ten semiannual coupon payments in each world. The proceeds difference arises once. For a like-date, five-year funding comparison, use R4's end-2025 3.87% USD and 8.09% GEL rates at constant FX and the bond's effective annual discount rate. This separate pricing exercise concerns the one documented issuance. It does not use future reserve purchases, all sovereign debt or a lifetime financing benefit.

## Household incidence and uncertainty

Compute $p_t=p_{t-12}(100+\pi^N_t)/(100+\pi^O_t)$ with pre-episode $p=1$. For matched survey-price spending $B_g$ and reported foreign receipts $T_g$ define a two-channel balance $B_g(1-p_t)-f_gT_g(1-e^N_t/e^O_t)$. Here $f_g$ is the share of group receipts retaining a fixed USD value. The counterfactual holds household quantities and baseline real exposures fixed. The full survey-price comparison uses the same 2024 price basis for both channels. A separate column revalues measured basket quantities to December 2024 category prices. Applying the aggregate price movement to each matched category is a calibration. Group-specific relative prices, unmatched goods, foreign-currency composition, sender responses and quantities could change the sign.

The alternative R5 column applies a retail change of one quarter of the proportional quote change. Scale the published diary-cluster standard errors only for the corresponding linear statistic and price basis. The matched December-valued and receipt components have separate uncertainty. The R5 quarter-pass/full-USD alternative has a joint error from the same household records. The unreported covariance for the new survey-price joint weights is left open. The calibrations have no coverage probability or estimated likelihood weights. [R5 definitions and design limits](../r5_2026_11_13/README.md)

## Reproduction and independent checks

The generator reads only tracked inputs, verifies their hashes and checks that each R3 dollar balance agrees with the reserve lot built from purchases and SOFR. Run from the root with the [existing Python environment](../../replication/requirements-r3-reopen.txt).

```sh
python src/r7_synthesis.py --check
python -m unittest discover -s tests -p test_r7_synthesis.py -v
python replication/check_repository.py
```

Omit `--check` to regenerate the twelve data tables and the chart. The numerical tests use separate equations and source joins, including independent per-lot compounding, closed-form first-event annuities, direct coupon pricing and reaggregation of all September tranches. The repository check also runs the prior research tests and verifies archive preservation. Same-session independent implementation is distinct from external peer replication. The [R6 audit](../r6_2026_11_15/AUDIT_2026_11_16.md) identified two problems with revised bond input handling. R7 uses the independently audited source bytes, rejects changes to those terms and passes the actual coupon frequency explicitly. Any new input vintage requires renewed source validation.
