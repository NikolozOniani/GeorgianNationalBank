# Evidence adequacy and empirical design

**Project:** The Economics of Reserve Accumulation in Georgia: An Independent Assessment of Costs, Benefits, and Optimal Reserve Adequacy.

**Author:** Nikoloz Oniani. **Assessment date:** 8 November 2026. **Audited baseline:** `54988b512af3a8373c5e80d1daaa12ba1fbae5f1`.

This is a research decision document, not a paper draft. Existing manuscripts and figures are preserved as historical working versions. They do not establish readiness for the newly specified reserve-adequacy study.

## Decision

**Proceed with a focused reserve-accounting and structural-sensitivity project. Do not yet estimate or announce Georgia's optimal reserve level.** The evidence supports descriptive financial accounting, partial stock-flow reconciliation and an audit of selected published equations. It is insufficient for a full reserve carry-cost estimate, a net welfare estimate, or replication of Cangul and Abel's reported optimum. Those are separate gates; failure at the last gate need not block the first two.

All 29 existing automated tests pass. The new independent audit additionally verifies pinned processed inputs, counts actual coverage and recomputes key accounting identities and the isolated dollarization calculation using Decimal arithmetic. Passing arithmetic tests does not validate an economic estimand, the original bank submissions, or a causal claim.

## 1. Evidence inventory and fitness for purpose

| Evidence | What exists | What it can establish | What it cannot establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly policy panel | 60 months, 2020–2024; policy rate, reserve components, official FX, CPI and interbank rates | Historical policy environment and reserve composition | Exogenous shocks, marginal financing cost or reserve effectiveness |
| Intervention observations | Two annual auction/BMatch net benchmarks and one verified auction event | Annual net intervention comparison | A complete event study or monthly executed-flow bridge |
| Unobserved panel fields | Monthly purchases/sales by channel, government FX, FX/gold valuation and other reserve movements are all blank | Explicit measurement of the acquisition gap | Zero activity or deliberate nondisclosure |
| External accounts | Twenty quarterly current-account and reserve-asset-flow observations | Quarterly external context; candidate transaction-versus-stock bridge | Monthly timing or a fully validated reserve revaluation account |
| Financial statements | 64 observations covering 2023–2024 in one report's comparative columns | Actual accrued income/expense and balance-sheet identities | Marginal cost of additional reserves or a five-year cost trend |
| Household credit/deposit panel | 46 series × 60 months | Retail exposure and nominal portfolio composition | Private-dollarization response to reserve accumulation; household welfare |
| Household survey pilot | Validated 2017 survey, incomplete price crosswalk | Limited historical price exposure | Current incidence of reserve costs or comprehensive inflation |
| ARA denominator | Quoted IMF coverage ratios only in this research block | Sensitivity to a stated reference ratio | Verified denominator, vintage-consistent reserve need in dollars, or an optimal target |
| Model inputs | Published PDF and selected explicit functional forms | Algebra and selected conditional arithmetic | Baseline, extensions and QIPF numerical replication |

`monthly_coverage.csv` is generated from the actual panel, not the source manifest's claims. The field named reconciliation status is populated text, not a measured financial series. Monthly rates with full coverage do not compensate for missing transaction data. The bank panel includes nonresidents and entrepreneurs in several tables; it must not be equated to resident private-sector dollarization without a population bridge.

The source vintages were downloaded in 2026. They are adequate for retrospective accounting if labeled consistently; they are not policymakers' real-time information sets. Existing 2020–2024 data also cannot establish the conditions surrounding the 2026 target without extending the reserve, GDP, liability and ARA inputs to the model's information date. Keep historical evaluation and contemporary target evaluation separate.

**Important strengthening of the previous critique:** Annex I's initial CRRA first-order condition and its later average-loss/reserves expression are not shown to be equivalent. The later dollarization welfare expression also omits an explicit direct consumption-smoothing benefit. Resolve the objective and derivation before changing one sign and numerically calling the result a corrected IMF model. Otherwise the researcher would be optimizing a different, inadequately specified model.

No lower and upper welfare bounds have been identified. Sensitivity grids are conditional scenarios, not partial identification unless their admissible parameter set and restrictions are separately justified. A literature-based crisis probability is a calibration, not a Georgian estimated frequency. Counting crises in five annual observations would be indefensible.

## 3. Reproducibility of Cangul and Abel

The official 2026 paper is archived with SHA-256 `5ef04855709e00663a1b63d7ee5f002d392e202da58e000e81c4af341bebf777`. Annex I is PDF pp.13–15 (printed pp.10–12). Current official publication/ARA pages were rechecked for this assessment. Targeted official-site searches did not identify a replication archive; that is a failed retrieval, **not proof that no code exists or that authors refuse access**. No request has been sent.

Reproducibility has four levels:

1. **Published arithmetic: partial pass.** Logarithmic dollarization/loss functions, derivative sign, spread-unit conversion and frozen-ratio arithmetic can be independently reproduced.
2. **Baseline optimum: not reproduced.** Require the exact baseline equation, definition of its intermediate price/probability term, complete calibration with units, time horizon, GDP/ARA conversion and feasible domain. The prose's notation is not a substitute for these inputs.
3. **Sovereign-spread and dollarization extensions: not reproduced.** Require the actual objective and first-order conditions, numerical values of initial opportunity costs and spreads, treatment of reserve-induced borrowing-cost savings, precise meaning of R, utility curvature, constraints and solver.
4. **FXI extension and combined target: not reproduced.** Require QIPF data and transformations, measurement of intervention, priors/posterior draws, shock covariance, intervention rule, reserve dynamics, simulation seed and horizon, drawdown definition/quantile, and aggregation/overlap rule. The reported simulation count is not an executable specification.

The abstract and conclusion use different contemporary reserve-coverage references (approximately 105% and 102%). Treat this as a vintage/rounding clarification request, not demonstrated fraud or a substantive contradiction. Record exact dates and denominators before comparison. The authors acknowledge overlapping stress needs; critique should test the adjustment, not claim they ignored overlap.

## 4. Economic framework to specify before estimation

Define the decision as the **incremental path and composition of usable FX reserves**, financed through explicitly named liabilities, conditional on the monetary/fiscal regime. Separate three accounting boundaries: NBG alone; consolidated NBG plus government; and the national economy including households, firms and banks. Domestic interest payments can be transfers within the national boundary but still have distributional and distortionary costs. Foreign interest and asset income have a different resource interpretation. Never add an NBG expense to a borrower's cost without checking double counting.

Define reserve services separately: liquidity in a sudden stop, potential reductions in crisis probability or severity, sovereign borrowing benefits, bank FX liquidity, and market-functioning intervention. These overlap. A single reserve dollar cannot be assumed to fund simultaneous independent withdrawals repeatedly. Model a joint stress scenario and accessible liquidity by horizon, with composition haircuts and policy constraints.

For a candidate reserve path R and parameters θ, compare expected discounted utility against an explicit alternative R0. Consumption must reflect funding costs, returns, crisis resources and distributional assumptions consistently. CRRA welfare, expected GDP loss and accounting net income are different objectives. Report consumption-equivalent differences only after defining baseline consumption, utility weights, discounting and horizon. Report an optimum as conditional on θ, and a range across defensible θ; do not attach a false statistical confidence interval.

Keep an independent break-even calculation beside the structural model: for an additional reserve tranche, what reduction in crisis probability/severity or sovereign spreads would cover its incremental cost? This exposes the required benefit without pretending that the response is identified. It can produce an original quantitative contribution even if the full IMF model remains inaccessible.

## 5. Sequenced empirical work and acceptance gates

### Work package 1 — construct reserve and financing accounts

Primary accounting window remains 2020–2024. Extend annual audited statements back through 2019 for opening positions and forward only where needed for the 2026 benchmark. Extract reserves by asset/currency, interest, realized gains, valuation, fees, monetary liabilities, government FX flows and operation balances. Reuse verified files. Search official tables and notes before requesting additional records.

Build separate stock, transaction, income and valuation ledgers. First test whether the already-held quarterly BOP reserve-asset transaction series can bridge the reserve-stock series: validate sign, coverage, SDR/gold treatment and revision vintage before subtraction. Its residual would be total other changes, not FX intervention. Then add channel-specific executed flows and government transactions. Do not infer missing monthly events from annual totals.

**Gate:** every included series has units, scope, date convention, source hash and missingness. Annual totals reconcile within source rounding; discrepancies above rounding are itemized, not forced to zero. Report the unreconciled proportion. If marginal funding cannot be allocated, release actual category expenses and a clearly conditional cost envelope, not a point estimate of sterilization cost.

### Work package 2 — reconstruct ARA and usable liquidity

Acquire the official ARA vintage used in the 2026 paper and its Georgian components: exports, broad money, short-term external debt on the required maturity basis, other liabilities and applicable exchange-rate-regime adjustments. Match nominal GDP, currencies and dates. Recover the authors' GDP/ARA conversion independently. The IMF ARA portal is a public acquisition lead, not a dataset already validated in this repository.

Build conventional metrics and an explicitly separate liquidity measure incorporating encumbrance, gold liquidity assumptions, reserve requirements, government liabilities and short-horizon drains. No automatic subtraction of all FX liabilities from gross reserves: stress accessibility and maturity determine the relevant adjustment.

**Gate:** reproduce published reserve/ARA ratios within documented rounding or explain each difference. Without this, no dollar-valued target gap or comparison of a GDP-ratio optimum to an ARA target is permitted.

### Work package 3 — replicate, then challenge the structural model

Prepare a precise request for code/calibration and interpretation of the sign/objective issues; do not send without authorization. Meanwhile transcribe the baseline into a unit-checked specification and independently derive first-order conditions. Use separate files for the published specification, any interpretation needed to make it executable, and an independently proposed model. Do not silently repair equations.

**Gate:** match published baseline and extension results to their reported precision, with complete parameter and solver logs, or explicitly mark nonreplication and its exact causes. Test numerical derivatives, global objective comparisons, boundary solutions, multiple roots and consumption positivity. A root of a first-order condition is not automatically a maximum. If code is unavailable, publish the independent model under its own name, not as an IMF replication.

### Work package 4 — original estimates and robust comparisons

Produce: (i) verified annual reserve-income/funding accounting; (ii) incremental cost envelopes by plausible financing mix and composition; (iii) break-even benefit surfaces; (iv) jointly stressed reserve needs with overlap sensitivities; and, only after model gates, (v) optimal reserve ranges with a decomposition by mechanism.

Evaluate low/central/high assumptions for funding, liquidity haircuts, crisis probability/duration, loss severity, reserve-use effectiveness, spread response and dollarization elasticity. Anchor ranges to documented evidence; distinguish estimated, calibrated and purely stress-test inputs. Avoid arbitrary Cartesian grids with economically impossible combinations. Identify which assumptions reverse the ranking of reserve paths and report the welfare surface's flatness: a precise optimizer can conceal an economically negligible difference.

For a causal spread or dollarization estimate, assemble a longer historical series, actual issuance costs or comparable secondary yields, reserve composition, global risk and domestic policy controls. A small Georgian time series or endogenous reserve-growth regression is insufficient. An external instrument or exposure design needs a defensible exclusion restriction; none is currently verified. Use labeled reduced-form associations or calibrations if that gate fails. No causal regression is the default requirement for the accounting contribution.
