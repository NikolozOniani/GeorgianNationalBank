# Editorial standard for the reserve-accumulation paper

Use this sheet when writing or reviewing *The Economics of Reserve Accumulation in Georgia: An Independent Assessment of Costs, Benefits, and Optimal Reserve Adequacy*, by Nikoloz Oniani. The [research protocol](../research/PROTOCOL.md) defines the economic question. The [manuscript structure](STRUCTURE.md) gives each section its purpose. This sheet governs the prose, citations, exhibits and presentation of the author’s review copy.

## 1. Which conventions apply

Economics journals publish their own instructions. We adopt the *American Economic Review* (AER) author–date and exhibit conventions, and the manuscript dimensions used by the *Review of Economic Studies*. The table records the journal rules checked on 26 November 2026. The editorial decisions in the rest of this sheet are the house style. For an actual submission, apply the chosen journal’s instructions for that stage.

## 2. What the reader sees

- **First page.** Set the study title exactly as registered, with Nikoloz Oniani directly beneath it, followed by the draft date and an author-confirmed affiliation or contact detail where applicable. Use one paragraph for the abstract. Follow it with keywords and relevant Journal of Economic Literature (JEL) codes. Use a page number in the footer. Omit decorative covers, logos, slogans and repository branding.
- **Abstract.** In at most 100 words, identify the episode, the comparison and the main answer. Attach the decisive assumption to a simulated number. If a benefit can only be valued through a break-even condition, say what would have to change for the purchases to pay. The abstract should stand alone without citations, promises or a table of contents.
- **Page and type.** Use A4, a single column, 25.4 mm margins, a 12-point Times-family serif face, 1.5-spaced body text and numbered pages. Use the same face and size for single-spaced references and footnotes. Number sections 1, 2, 3 and subsections 2.1, 2.2. Use sentence case for headings. Keep short headings with their first paragraph. These are review-copy choices.
- **End matter.** Place acknowledgments, confirmed funding and competing interests, and a short data-and-code availability statement after the conclusion, followed by references. Letter any in-paper appendices A, B and so on. Identify substantive AI assistance with the tool, its use and the checks the author actually made. The author takes responsibility for the analysis and wording. Supply declarations from the author’s actual circumstances. [AEA accepted-article guidance](https://www.aeaweb.org/journals/aer/accepted-article-guidelines) · [Oxford Journals AI use and disclosure policy](https://academic.oup.com/pages/for-authors/journals/preparing-and-submitting-your-manuscript/ai-principles-for-oxford-journals/policy-on-ai-use-and-disclosure-for-oxford-journals-authors)

## 3. How the argument should read

Open with the Georgian purchase episode and the economic choice facing the country. Explain why observing purchases alongside appreciation leaves the no-purchases exchange rate unknown. Give the principal finding and the assumption on which it turns early enough for a reader to challenge both. Use the closest prior work to explain what this paper adds. Bring in other literature where it supplies a mechanism, parameter or competing interpretation. For a published example, Bianchi, Hatchondo, and Martinez introduce a specific tradeoff between liquid reserves and debt repayment and identify the debt-maturity assumption their result needs. [Article and abstract](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.20140443) · [Cochrane’s writing guidance for economists](https://www.johnhcochrane.com/research-all/writing-tips-for-phd-studentsnbsp)

Give each paragraph one economic job. Lead with the claim, present the observation or reasoning that earns it, then explain what it changes for the question at hand. Name the actor or the variable. Use “I” for a consequential choice made by the sole author, such as selecting a comparison. Use the institution’s name for its actions and the model as the subject of its predictions. Reserve Nikoloz Oniani’s name for the byline, declarations and genuine bibliographic references. Use American English throughout.

**A cut that improves the paper.** Delete “These findings underscore the complex and multifaceted nature of reserve accumulation.” If the point concerns liquidity, identify the payment due, when it falls due and which assets are available at that date. If the paragraph still adds nothing to either clause of the research question, cut it. Use the same test for a historical detour, an extra citation or a second summary of an exhibit.

End with the answer to each clause at the strength the evidence permits. State the alternative assumption most capable of reversing the assessment. A policy recommendation belongs in the author’s voice and requires a stated economic objective. Keep project history, filenames, GitHub commentary, commands, validation logs and instructions to reviewers in the replication materials. When a Georgian summary accompanies the paper, carry over the English text’s population, units, benchmark and degree of certainty.

## 4. Citations a reader can verify

Use the AER variant of Chicago author–date. Place the citation directly after the proposition it supports, before the sentence-ending punctuation. Name a researcher in the sentence when their particular model, finding or assumption matters. For supporting background, use a parenthetical citation, for example (Cangul and Abel 2026, para. 13). Cite the producing institution for its statistics and the named authors for a signed study. Refer to this paper’s own result by table, figure or equation number. [AER style guide, text citations and references](https://www.aeaweb.org/journals/aer/style-guide)

- In text, name all authors through four. For five or more, use the first surname and “et al.” Alphabetize references by author and order works by the same authors by year. Use matching a/b suffixes for same-author, same-year entries. In the bibliography, give all names through ten authors and the first seven followed by “et al.” for longer lists.
- For every quotation, numerical attribution or contested interpretation, open the original and check the passage, definitions, period, units and qualifications. Add a printed page, paragraph, equation, table or series identifier that will take a reader to the evidence. A DOI that resolves establishes the identity of the work. Reading the cited passage establishes whether it supports the sentence. Narrow or remove a claim whose source fails that test.
- A journal article entry gives the authors, year, article title, journal, volume, issue, pages and DOI where available. For a dataset, give the creator, dissemination year or “n.d.” if unknown, dataset and series title, release or version, distributor and DOI or source URL. Supply the access date for a URL without a DOI. Distinguish the publication date from the observation period. For an author calculation, name the input data and explain the transformation in the exhibit note. Give the exact vintage and reproduction steps in the companion materials. [AEA data-reference instructions](https://www.aeaweb.org/journals/data/references)

## 5. Numbers, equations and exhibits

| Item | House rule |
|---|---|
| Quantities | Give the object, date or horizon, unit, denominator and comparison. Define Georgian lari (GEL) and US dollars (USD) on first use. For GEL per USD, a lower number means a stronger lari. A percentage change in USD per GEL uses the reciprocal series and its own denominator. Use percentage points for differences between rates and percent for proportional changes. Round once from full precision. |
| Status of a result | Use “reported” for a source observation, “equals” for an accounting identity, “estimated” for a parameter fitted to data and “assumed” for a calibration. Name the scenario when reporting a model result. Use causal language about the historical episode only to the extent the research design identifies the effect. Explain what a range varies. Reserve confidence intervals for an inferential procedure that produces them. Report a sensitivity that erases or reverses the headline result beside it. |
| Economic interpretation | Identify gross reserves, usable assets and net intervention separately. A stress threshold covers specified drains over a specified horizon. A welfare optimum requires an objective and marginal costs and benefits. A break-even risk reduction describes how large the benefit must be. Each quantity merits its own label. |
| Equations | Typeset mathematics. Define each new symbol, time index, unit and sign at first use. State the benchmark and direction of subtraction. Number displayed equations consecutively on the left, with (A1), (A2) in Appendix A. Explain the economic implication in the next sentence. Put secondary derivations in an appendix. |
| Tables | Number tables in order of first mention. Put a descriptive title above, units in column headings, and explanatory notes followed by a source note below. Use horizontal rules sparingly and align comparable numbers at the decimal. Keep a short table and its notes on one page. Distinguish online Table S1 from main Table 1. |
| Figures | Give each figure one numbered caption, dates, units, labeled axes and a legend where needed. Use line styles as well as color for observed and simulated paths. Explain any band, panel-specific scale or differing horizon. Use vector graphics for charts. Check labels at ordinary print size and in grayscale. |

Keep the economic mechanism, material identifying or calibration assumptions, main results and decisive sensitivity in the article. Put supporting derivations, data definitions and additional robustness in appendices. A short availability statement points to the exact version of the replication package and explains access restrictions where applicable. Technical instructions and hashes belong with the package. [AER guidance on exhibits](https://www.aeaweb.org/journals/aer/style-guide) · [AER guidance on replication and supplements](https://www.aeaweb.org/journals/aer/accepted-article-guidelines)

## 6. Editorial check before typesetting

1. Read the abstract, introduction and conclusion together. Check that all three give the same comparison, direction of effect and degree of certainty. Remove any stronger claim introduced by a heading, caption or translation.
2. Write a short margin note for each paragraph giving its contribution to the argument. Cut duplicated notes and relocate technical instructions. Read the prose aloud to catch inflated language, unexplained acronyms and abrupt changes of subject.
3. Check citations in both directions, including exhibit notes. Match every citation to a reference and every reference to a use. Open the cited passage or series and compare it with the exact claim. Verify authors, titles, dates, locators and identifiers against the publisher.
4. Trace each headline number to the displayed exhibit and its underlying output. Check the unit, sign, rounding, denominator, benchmark and sensitivity. Read each table and figure on its own.
5. Check the byline, disclosures, numbering and availability statement against the version being circulated. At proof stage, inspect page breaks, footnotes, legends and the placement of each source note.
