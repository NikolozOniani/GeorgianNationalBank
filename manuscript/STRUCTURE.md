# The Economics of Reserve Accumulation in Georgia: An Independent Assessment of Costs, Benefits, and Optimal Reserve Adequacy

**Author:** Nikoloz Oniani

**Research question:** Did the National Bank of Georgia's large-scale foreign-currency purchases prevent the Georgian lari from appreciating, and were the resulting economic costs justified by the benefits of reserve accumulation?

**Scope:** One intervention episode, one economic counterfactual, and its consequences.

## The argument of the paper

Organize the paper around the decision to buy the next dollar of reserves. Establish what Georgia bought and how the lari moved. Compare that history with a suspension of purchases. Then ask what the extra dollars could have paid for in a crisis, what they cost to carry, and how large an insurance benefit would justify them. The original contribution is the ability to follow the **same monthly purchases** through these comparisons. Focus on the increase in September gross reserves from 5 billion US dollars (USD) under the intermediate policy to the observed USD 8.167 billion. Its value depends on when the dollars were bought and which obligations they could settle.

The six sections and their exhibits might occupy 25–30 pages. Use fewer pages when the argument is complete and more when a reader needs the detail. Write the uncited abstract after the conclusion. In up to about 100 words, give the episode, the no-purchases comparison, one result with its decisive assumption and the condition under which the extra insurance pays.

### 1. Introduction

Open with the apparent puzzle. Between December 31, 2024 and September 30, 2026, the National Bank of Georgia (NBG) purchased USD 5.803 billion net, while one lari came to buy 7.74 percent more dollars. The first question concerns **further appreciation**. The second concerns the value Georgia received for accumulating reserves. Preview one finding on each. Tell the reader immediately that all three principal currency scenarios assume that buying dollars initially weakens the lari and that the Georgian evidence leaves the achieved reduction in crisis risk unresolved.

Position the contribution against the closest alternatives. A 2025 study for the International Monetary Fund (IMF) asks whether intervention can stabilize a shallow market. Cangul and Abel’s 2026 model asks what stock of reserves maximizes welfare under stated assumptions. This paper evaluates particular purchases on particular dates and identifies the point at which additional dollars stop covering a specified set of payments. Develop each comparison where it enters the analysis.

### 3. The lari without purchases

Start with the identification problem. Strong private inflows can both appreciate the lari and induce the NBG to buy. Historical Georgian purchase auctions show appreciation before intervention, and the estimated association changes sign across specifications. Explain why the paper uses a calibrated response and disclose that the choice followed inspection of earlier unsuccessful models.

Show how a purchase, scaled by prior-year dollar GDP, affects the exchange rate on impact and how that effect decays. Attribute the cross-country evidence to Adler, Lisack, and Mano (2015, Table A11, column 4). The central case assumes 0.9565 log percentage points of depreciation per purchase equal to 1 percent of annual GDP, half the transferred impact magnitude, and a ten-month half-life. Explain how inflation feeds back into policy rates and hence the exchange rate. Compare the September central quote at the observed policy rate (2.3481 lari per dollar) with the quote when rates respond. This isolates monetary feedback within the model.

Figure 1 should align the monthly purchases with the observed quote and the three conditional paths. At the September endpoint the observed quote was 2.6051 Georgian lari (GEL) per US dollar. The central feedback case gives 2.3558, or 10.58 percent more dollars per lari than observed that month. Calculate this gain by dividing the observed quote by the simulated quote, subtracting one and multiplying by 100. Its **average monthly** gain is 4.70 percent. Give the conservative and aggressive endpoint gains (2.69 and 26.97 percent) beside the zero-impact and reversed-sign results (2.6051 and 2.8808 GEL per dollar). Assign no probability weights to the paths. Address the competing stabilization mechanism in Abel, Huang, Kolasa, and Linde (2025, paras. 23–29), who studied the 2020–2024 episode.

Close with one consequence of the quote convention. In the central comparison a USD 100 invoice costs GEL 24.93 less, while a recipient converting USD 100 receives GEL 24.93 less. The same exchange-rate change has opposite implications for these two positions. Use the corrected household survey in the appendix for partial basket and transfer exposures. The crosswalk leaves 24.67 percent of 2024 survey consumption unmatched to the selected price categories. The currency of the transfers is unknown.

### 4. How much protection did the reserves buy?

Start with an obligation and its due date. Distinguish binding public payments from a discretionary replay of pandemic foreign-currency sales and from a separate, eligible bank claim. Compare peak cumulative drains with assets deliverable by that date. At the September cutoff the no-purchases portfolio falls USD 188.60 million short on twelve months of scheduled net public drains, counting deposits and securities at par and assuming no outside financing. Adding the import-scaled sales replay raises the gap to USD 2.632 billion. Test access to gold and securities and the availability of Treasury financing. The historical payment tests reproduced scheduled bills and tied a simple debt-service benchmark. Use that result to delimit what those tests validate.

Make Figure 2 the central exhibit. Vary the share of purchases retained and show the remaining twelve-month shortfall against September gross holdings. Under the at-par, import-scaled pandemic-sales replay plus public dues, USD 4 billion leaves USD 841 million uncovered. At USD 4.841 billion that gap closes. Moving from USD 5 billion to USD 8.167 billion closes no further gap for that September test, but reduces the same shortfall at **11 earlier month-ends**. These overlapping stresses cannot be added. Show the joint stress on the same figure. A 5 percent securities haircut and a distinct USD 100 million bank call raise the gross requirement to USD 5.083 billion.

Use Table 2 to hold the September stock fixed at USD 5 billion and vary acquisition timing. Earliest, proportional and latest eligible purchases give central endpoint quotes of 2.4287, 2.4694 and 2.5108 GEL per USD. The twelve-month replay leaves a shortfall at 4, 12 and 18 of the 21 month-ends respectively. These retrospective schedules show why a terminal stock alone cannot describe the policy that produced it.

Compare the IMF benchmark with the cash test **at matched dates**. At end-2025 the two reconstructions of gross reserve coverage give 96.70 percent using M2 and 90.68 percent using M3 in the broad-money component of the IMF’s Assessing Reserve Adequacy metric. A matched September 2026 denominator is unavailable. Cangul and Abel (2026, para. 13) obtain 145–150 percent of that metric at an assumed 10 percent crisis probability. Explain the broader services their objective values, including sovereign borrowing costs and private dollarization, and the possible overlap with intervention liquidity (para. 12). The independent reconstruction checks components and published equations. The authors’ numerical optimum remains unreproduced.

### 5. What would justify the cost of additional reserves?

Keep the central bank’s interest account distinct from the national return on the dollars. Start with audited whole-portfolio income, then price only the incremental holdings. Explain the use of the US secured overnight financing rate as a proxy for the dollar yield, how the GEL counterpart is financed and how closing revaluation is accounted for separately. With half the marginal GEL counterpart paid the policy rate and half earning zero, modeled episode net interest expense is GEL 28.68 million. Interest cost changes sign at a remunerated share of 46.39 percent. The actual marginal funding mix is unknown. Interest paid to residents can be a domestic transfer.

Use Table 3 to determine how much the purchases would have to lower expected national crisis losses to cover the return on a feasible alternative use of the dollars. Put opportunity cost and first-crisis losses in December 2024 purchasing power. Charge the forgone return over acquisition and a five-year continuation, state the terminal convention, and count the first loss once. Suppose a feasible external alternative yields one percentage point more per year after adjusting for risk. At a 3 percent real discount rate, a USD 3.81 billion first-crisis loss and 10 percent annual crisis risk under suspension, retaining all purchases must reduce that annual risk by **2.55 percentage points** to break even. Compare assumed reductions of one and three percentage points. The first leaves a shortfall against the opportunity cost and the second yields a surplus.

Give the marginal decision its own row. From USD 5 billion to USD 8.167 billion the required annual reduction is **1.39 percentage points**, conditional on 10 percent risk **at the USD 5 billion policy**. Each comparison assumes its own starting risk, so the reductions across rows cannot be added. Distinguish this required reduction from the unknown reduction actually delivered. Discuss the January 2026 sovereign issue briefly as evidence for a separate financing channel. Only reserves accumulated before pricing could affect its proceeds. Keep overlapping insurance, financing and household effects out of a summed welfare total.

### 6. Conclusion

Answer the clauses in order. The observed lari appreciated. Under all three principal calibrations, purchases restrained further appreciation. The size and even the sign of that effect depend on the assumed intervention response. Additional reserves cover identifiable obligations under the stated stresses. Whether the last dollars justified their cost depends on the attainable external return and the crisis losses they avert. Close by distinguishing a dated cash-coverage threshold from a welfare optimum. Ranking the stocks requires evidence on how expected losses change with holdings, their marginal national cost and the alternatives actually available to Georgia.
