# The Economics of Reserve Accumulation in Georgia: An Independent Assessment of Costs, Benefits, and Optimal Reserve Adequacy

**Author:** Nikoloz Oniani

**Research question:** Did the National Bank of Georgia's large-scale foreign-currency purchases prevent the Georgian lari from appreciating, and were the resulting economic costs justified by the benefits of reserve accumulation?

**Scope:** One intervention episode, one economic counterfactual, and its consequences.

## The argument of the paper

Organize the paper around the decision to buy the next dollar of reserves. Establish what Georgia bought and how the lari moved. Compare that history with a suspension of purchases. Then ask what the extra dollars could have paid for in a crisis, what they cost to carry, and how large an insurance benefit would justify them. The original contribution is the ability to follow the **same monthly purchases** through these comparisons. Focus on the increase in September gross reserves from 5 billion US dollars (USD) under the intermediate policy to the observed USD 8.167 billion. Its value depends on when the dollars were bought and which obligations they could settle.

Use the six section headings below in the review copy. The [research protocol](../research/PROTOCOL.md) defines the experiment and the [editorial standard](STYLE.md) governs presentation. Allow roughly 25–30 pages for the six sections including their exhibits. Within the 45-page ceiling, allow room for the title and abstract, declarations, references and any in-paper appendix. The uncited abstract has at most 100 words. Give the episode, the no-purchases comparison, one result with its decisive assumption, and the condition under which the extra insurance pays. Write it after the conclusion. Target 95 words, allocating 20 to the episode and question, 20 to the purchase-suspension comparison, 30 to one simulated result and the assumption determining its sign, and 25 to the insurance break-even.

### 1. Introduction

#### 1.1 Purchases during an appreciation

1. **90 words.** Open with the National Bank of Georgia’s (NBG) reported net purchases of USD 5.803 billion and its two official quotes, 2.8068 Georgian lari (GEL) per US dollar on December 31, 2024, and 2.6051 on September 30, 2026.
2. **110 words.** Derive the 7.74 percent increase in dollars per lari from those quotes. Pose the two questions. Did purchases restrain further appreciation? Was the additional protection worth its economic cost?

#### 1.2 Principal findings and their limits

1. **150 words.** Preview the purchase-suspension comparison. Disclose that all three principal currency scenarios assume buying dollars initially weakens the lari, and identify the result when that effect is set to zero.
2. **150 words.** Preview the cash a specified reserve tranche could supply and the reduction in crisis risk required to justify its cost. Identify the risk reduction actually achieved as unknown.

#### 1.3 Contribution relative to prior work

1. **125 words.** Introduce the shallow-market stabilization mechanism in Abel, Huang, Kolasa, and Linde (2025). Explain why a central bank might intervene during appreciation and where Section 3 tests that interpretation.
2. **125 words.** Introduce Cangul and Abel’s (2026) model of optimal reserve holdings. State the episode-specific contribution of following the same dated purchases through the exchange rate, financing account and obligations payable.

### 2. The purchase episode and the policy comparison

#### 2.1 What the transaction records measure

1. **160 words.** Distinguish dated auctions, monthly net disclosures from BMatch (the electronic trading platform), and the dates official exchange-rate quotes take effect. Identify the execution dates and prices that the public records disclose.
2. **190 words.** In Table 1, reconcile minimum purchases of USD 5,819.35 million and minimum sales of USD 16.20 million to recorded net purchases of USD 5,803.15 million. Use the March 2026 net sale to explain the signs and why additional offsetting trades can disappear from monthly net figures.

#### 2.2 From intervention to the reserve stock

1. **140 words.** Trace calendar 2025 market purchases and government foreign-currency transactions through the balance of payments. Reconcile the transaction account to the change in gross reserves at the dates and frequency the sources support.
2. **110 words.** Account for gold valuation, quantify the remaining discrepancy and distinguish annual or quarterly entries from the monthly components that remain unavailable.

#### 2.3 Definition of the policy experiment

1. **170 words.** Start both economies with the December 31, 2024 balance sheet. Over the following 21 months, remove the minimum gross purchase leg and retain the common sales in the reference simulation. Identify September 2026 as the last observed purchase month in this vintage.
2. **180 words.** Specify common external shocks, information available at each decision and the rules governing emergency sales, interest rates and funding. Define intermediate policies as fractions of the same monthly purchases. This definition carries through the currency, financing and insurance calculations.

### 3. The lari without purchases

#### 3.1 What Georgian intervention data identify

1. **180 words.** Explain how private inflows can strengthen the lari and induce the NBG to buy dollars. Use appreciation before historical purchase auctions to document selection into intervention.
2. **220 words.** Present the fitted Georgian purchase coefficients, their sampling uncertainty and the sign change across samples. Explain why the earlier model failed its prespecified validation test. Disclose that the choice to calibrate the currency response followed inspection of these results.

#### 3.2 Purchase impact, persistence, and monetary feedback

1. **200 words.** Attribute the cross-country estimate to Adler, Lisack, and Mano (2015, Table A11, column 4). Explain scaling by prior-year dollar GDP and the transfer to Georgia. The central case assumes 0.9565 log percentage points of depreciation on impact for purchases of 1 percent of annual GDP, half the published impact, and a ten-month half-life.
2. **200 words.** Trace the response from the exchange rate to expected inflation, the policy rate and back to the exchange rate. Isolate monetary feedback by comparing the central September quote at the observed policy-rate path (2.3481 GEL per USD) with the quote when rates respond. Identify the feedback assumptions.

#### 3.3 Monthly paths, endpoint, and average effect

1. **140 words.** Align monthly purchases and all three simulated paths with the official quotes in Figure 1. Place the observed September quote of 2.6051 GEL per USD beside the central simulated quote of 2.3558 under suspension. Divide the observed quote by the simulated quote, subtract one and multiply by 100 to obtain the percentage gain in dollars per lari.
2. **140 words.** Report the conservative, central and aggressive endpoint gains of 2.69, 10.58 and 26.97 percent in dollars per lari. Distinguish the central endpoint from the 4.70 percent mean of the monthly gains. The scenarios carry no probability weights.

#### 3.4 What erases or reverses the currency result?

1. **110 words.** Place the zero-impact and reversed-sign cases beside the principal paths. They imply 2.6051 and 2.8808 GEL per USD, respectively. Explain how confidence associated with higher reserves could offset the assumed direct purchase effect.
2. **110 words.** Vary persistence and the impact of repeated large purchases. Revisit temporary overshooting and the competing stabilization mechanism in Abel, Huang, Kolasa, and Linde (2025, paras. 23–29), who studied 2020–2024.

#### 3.5 Who gains or loses from a stronger lari?

1. **100 words.** Use a USD 100 invoice and a USD 100 transfer. In the central September simulation the payer spends GEL 24.93 less and the recipient obtains GEL 24.93 less on conversion. Identify the currency position in each case.
2. **100 words.** Connect the example to the corrected household survey. Report the 24.67 percent share of 2024 consumption unmatched to the selected prices and the unknown currency of transfers. Direct readers to the group-level results in Appendix D.

### 4. How much protection did the reserves buy?

#### 4.1 Payments due and assets deliverable

1. **175 words.** Identify the payer, due date and currency of each scheduled public obligation. Introduce discretionary foreign-currency sales and an eligible bank claim as distinct uses, with an explicit check for overlap.
2. **175 words.** Compare peak cumulative calls at one, three and twelve months with assets deliverable by each date. The historical payment tests reproduced scheduled bills and tied a simple scheduled-debt-service benchmark. Use that result to delimit what the tests validate.

#### 4.2 The September coverage threshold

1. **135 words.** Begin with public dues alone. Counting deposits and securities at par and assuming no outside financing, the no-purchases portfolio has a twelve-month shortfall of USD 188.60 million at the September cutoff.
2. **165 words.** Add an import-scaled replay of pandemic foreign-currency sales. The modeled gap under suspension rises to USD 2.632 billion. In Figure 2, vary the retained share of purchases against September gross holdings. USD 4 billion leaves USD 841 million uncovered. Locate the USD 4.841 billion threshold and identify the obligations it covers.

#### 4.3 Asset access and simultaneous claims

1. **115 words.** Vary the availability of gold, the amount realizable from securities and access to Treasury financing. Explain which change affects the resources deliverable by the payment date.
2. **135 words.** On Figure 2, combine a 5 percent securities haircut with a distinct eligible USD 100 million bank call. Explain why the gross requirement rises to USD 5.083 billion and what the USD 5 billion portfolio can settle under this joint stress.

#### 4.4 Protection before September

1. **100 words.** Repeat the twelve-month public-payment and import-scaled sales stress at successive month-ends. Identify the first shortfall and the change in its size as the share of purchases retained increases.
2. **100 words.** Compare the proportional USD 5 billion policy with the full purchase path ending at the observed USD 8.167 billion. The latter closes no further gap in this September test and reduces shortfalls at 11 earlier month-ends. Explain why overlapping stress windows cannot be summed.

#### 4.5 Acquisition timing at a common terminal stock

1. **125 words.** Construct earliest, proportional and latest eligible purchase schedules, all ending with USD 5 billion gross in September. Explain the retrospective use of the terminal target and the restriction to purchase opportunities available in each month.
2. **125 words.** In Table 2 compare their 4, 12 and 18 exposed month-ends out of 21 under the twelve-month replay. Give the corresponding central September quotes of 2.4287, 2.4694 and 2.5108 GEL per USD and compare episode financing expenses.

#### 4.6 Comparison with the IMF reserve-adequacy benchmark

1. **150 words.** Match dates and denominators. At end-2025, the reconstructed ratios are 96.70 percent using M2 and 90.68 percent using M3 in the broad-money component of the IMF’s Assessing Reserve Adequacy metric. A matched September 2026 denominator is unavailable.
2. **150 words.** Explain the 145–150 percent of the metric obtained by Cangul and Abel (2026, para. 13) at an assumed 10 percent crisis probability. Discuss sovereign borrowing costs, private dollarization and overlap with intervention liquidity (para. 12). Identify the components and published equations checked independently and the numerical optimum that remains unreproduced.

### 5. What would justify the cost of additional reserves?

#### 5.1 The NBG’s incremental interest account

1. **180 words.** Start with audited whole-portfolio income. In Table 3 price the additional monthly holdings using the US secured overnight financing rate as a proxy dollar yield, then trace alternative GEL funding sources. Account for closing currency revaluation separately.
2. **170 words.** Report the modeled GEL 28.68 million net interest expense when half the marginal GEL counterpart earns the policy rate and the remainder earns zero. Interest cost changes sign at a remunerated share of 46.39 percent. Identify the unknown marginal funding mix, consolidation with the Treasury, and interest payments that transfer income to residents.

#### 5.2 The national opportunity cost and the first crisis loss

1. **225 words.** Define a feasible external alternative earning an assumed one percentage point more each year after adjusting for risk. Charge the forgone return over acquisition and a five-year continuation. Put costs and benefits in December 2024 purchasing power.
2. **225 words.** Introduce the assumed USD 3.81 billion first-crisis loss and 3 percent real discount rate. Count the first loss once. State the reference assumptions of zero additional pre-cutoff crisis benefit and zero residual resource gain after the five-year horizon.

#### 5.3 Break-even for the full purchase policy

1. **100 words.** At an assumed 10 percent annual crisis risk under suspension, derive the 2.55 percentage-point annual reduction required for the full purchase policy to break even under the stated return, loss and discount assumptions.
2. **100 words.** Compare assumed annual risk reductions of one and three percentage points. The first leaves a shortfall against opportunity cost and the second yields a surplus. Identify the achieved reduction as unknown.

#### 5.4 Break-even for holdings above USD 5 billion

1. **100 words.** Reset the assumed starting annual crisis risk to 10 percent at the USD 5 billion policy. Compare the additional national cost of reaching the observed USD 8.167 billion stock with the loss reduction required for that increment.
2. **100 words.** Give the marginal decision its own row in Table 3. Report the required annual risk reduction of 1.39 percentage points. Explain why the full-policy and marginal hurdles have different starting policies and cannot be added.

#### 5.5 Sovereign financing and overlapping benefits

1. **75 words.** Use the January 2026 sovereign issue to illustrate the borrowing-cost channel. Only reserves accumulated before pricing can enter the comparison of issue proceeds.
2. **75 words.** Identify where issuance proceeds, replacement financing, cash protection and avoided crisis losses value the same service. Keep overlapping amounts and partial household exposures out of a summed national welfare account.

### 6. Conclusion

#### 6.1 Did purchases restrain further appreciation?

1. **60 words.** Return to the observed appreciation. State the direction of the simulated difference under each of the three principal purchase-response calibrations.
2. **65 words.** Identify the intervention-response assumption that determines the sign and the Georgian evidence needed to distinguish it from zero or the opposite sign.

#### 6.2 When were additional reserves worth their cost?

1. **85 words.** State which dated obligations the additional reserves cover in the specified stresses and why the answer changes with the timing of purchases and access to reserve assets.
2. **90 words.** Close with the evidence required to rank reserve stocks by national welfare. Specify a feasible alternative return, the marginal national cost and the change in expected crisis losses caused by an additional reserve dollar. Give the dated cash-coverage threshold its own interpretation.
