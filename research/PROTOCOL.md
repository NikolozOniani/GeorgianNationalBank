# Research protocol

**Study:** The Economics of Reserve Accumulation in Georgia: An Independent Assessment of Costs, Benefits, and Optimal Reserve Adequacy

**Author:** Nikoloz Oniani

**Fixed research question:** Did the National Bank of Georgia's large-scale foreign-currency purchases prevent the Georgian lari from appreciating, and were the resulting economic costs justified by the benefits of reserve accumulation?

**Fixed scope:** One intervention episode, one economic counterfactual, and its consequences.

## Decision of 24 November 2026

The primary method is a calibrated structural policy evaluation using the public Georgian records already assembled. Evaluate the removal of discretionary market purchases from 1 January 2025 through 30 September 2026, with both economies starting on 31 December 2024. September is the final intervention observation in the registered vintage. The question concerns these 21 months even if purchases continued. Earlier years supply evidence about transmission and stress.

This dated amendment follows inspection of the Georgian episode, the [rejected November experiment](../reports/r3_reopen_2026_11_16/README.md), the [Georgian purchase evidence](../reports/r3_fx_response/README.md), the [MPC tests](../reports/r3_rate_rule/README.md) and the [reserve holdouts](../reports/buffer_validation/README.md). It changes what can be released from a **new** simulation. The earlier candidates retain their original decisions, including the rejection of 1.8503 GEL/USD under the registered 90% resampling rule. Publish the new calibration before computing its episode paths. Disclose any subsequent change, its rationale and the old result alongside the revision. This is retrospective research.

## 1. Observed intervention and the policy experiment

Use the R1 monthly purchase-positive ledger, including net-sale months. Executed purchases and sales are separate economic actions. For each instrument with only a signed net observation n, the minimum-gross convention gives purchases max(n,0) and sales max(−n,0). Add the same nonnegative two-way amount to each leg to test ambiguity while preserving the reported net. Keep directly observed gross legs and apply the two-way adjustment only to unknown legs. Size the alternative from comparable gross disclosures where available, or show the reversal threshold conditional on an explicit amount. Preserve native reporting frequency, GDP scaling and trade versus disclosure dates. Government transactions, valuation and portfolio reallocations have their own accounts. [Episode registration](R0_REGISTRATION.md) · [R1 accounting](../reports/r1_2026_11_10/README.md)

Remove the modeled gross purchase leg. Both paths face the same external shocks, initial balance sheet and nonpurchase policy rules. In the alternative economy recalculate emergency sales, policy rates, reserve income, funding and prices when their triggers change. Specify how private flows absorb orders, what agents expect about future purchases and policy, what observed inflows are treated as common shocks, and which reserve assets can settle payments. For an unexpected suspension, agents may learn about it from the opening. A fully anticipated suspension is a separate expectations sensitivity on the same policy experiment. State how future shocks are forecast at each date. Realized future flows cannot enter the central bank's contemporaneous decision rule.

Report monthly E in GEL/USD and 100 × (E observed / E without purchases − 1), the percentage increase in USD per lari under the alternative. Report the endpoint, the average of the monthly gaps, and sensitivity to purchase persistence. Label each separately. Primary flows and resources remain in original currency wherever the source permits. Use the [R0 conventions](R0_REGISTRATION.md) for dating, real purchasing power and discounting.

The columns are reference cases for the strength of each mechanism. The central column is a working reference without a probability weight. Cross the assumptions across columns when conclusions depend on their combination. A large exchange-rate effect can coexist with small reserve benefits or the reverse.

**FX.** Adler, Lisack and Mano, Table A11 column 4, report a nominal bilateral impact of 1.913 in our sign convention and a 20-month half-life. It is a cross-country estimate with a balance-sheet intervention proxy and a trend-GDP denominator. Our aggressive case transfers those two empirical moments. The quarter and half impact weights and shorter durations are deliberate attenuation assumptions. They reflect uncertainty about market depth, timing and gross flows in Georgia. There is no estimated Georgian discount. Use the locked pre-episode annual USD GDP denominator and show the existing alternative scale. Approximate the direct impulse after h months by impact × 2^(−h/half-life). This approximates two moments of the published distributed-lag model. Keep its full lag profile as a comparator. Run zero direct impact and faster decay separately. Flag extrapolation of repeated large purchases beyond the source's support and show a diminishing-impact sensitivity. [Published estimates and Table A11](https://www.imf.org/external/pubs/ft/wp/2015/wp15130.pdf) · [Locked source coefficients](r3_reopen_calibration.json)

**Rates and inflation.** Use NBG's forward-looking, smoothed reaction-rule form. The three response coefficients and a reference quarterly smoothing weight of 0.75 are calibration choices. A monthly implementation must preserve quarterly decay and actual decision opportunities. Hold other rule inputs common in a partial-equilibrium specification or explicitly model their differences. Feed the alternative inflation forecast into the rule. Compare against a fixed-rate-path sensitivity and the failed historical MPC fit. The IMF Georgia estimate is about +0.5 pp of inflation at two years after a 10% depreciation. The lower cases allow appreciation to transmit less strongly than depreciation, as NBG describes. The upper case tests symmetry. Using a risk-sentiment shock to discipline a purchase shock is an additional transfer assumption. Match the response at eight quarters, report the assumed lag profile and test a faster profile. Use the figure's inflation units, distinguish the inflation rate from the price level, and label any response beyond September 2026 as a projection. Match the system's total inflation response under the reference policy rule before varying that rule. Counting its embedded monetary response twice would exaggerate the feedback. [NBG policy rule](https://nbg.gov.ge/en/page/monetary-policy-reaction-function) · [NBG monetary policy manual, paragraph 117](https://nbg.gov.ge/fm/მონეტარული_პოლიტიკა/დოკუმენტები/mp-manual-final-eng221021.pdf?v=7s9g9) · [IMF Georgia, paragraph 19 and Figure 13](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2025/english/1geoea2025002-source-pdf.pdf)

**Insurance.** Use the Step 2 rules for who pays, when receipts can be used and which assets can settle. At each month-end report the first uncovered payment and the extra protection that each reserve tranche buys at 1, 3 and 12 months. Where the bank shortfall, payer or cash date is unknown, give a conditional shortfall or the threshold that would exhaust available resources. Price disjoint protection at a feasible replacement-finance cost where documented. For the separate borrowing-cost channel compare zero, half and full transfer of the published reserve–spread slope to eligible new issuance using R6's bond cash flows. Apply each yield change only to the amount actually issued under the assumed financing plan. Keep this channel separate if it prices the same crisis loss. Otherwise report the loss or change in crisis probability required for the extra reserves to pay for themselves. Cross financing access, security haircuts and reserve benefits independently of FX persistence. The IMF's 10% crisis probability and 145–150% of ARA are published benchmark assumptions/results. The R6 hazard/loss choices are stress inputs. Neither supplies observed Georgian event probabilities. ## 3. Evidence and interpretation

Label each material input as **observed** (source, vintage, coverage and units), **estimated** (population, sample, uncertainty and specification) or **calibrated** (source moment, transfer or judgment, rationale and alternative). A cross-country estimate becomes a calibration when applied to Georgia. Identify the treatment convention, sales rule, funding mix, expectations, price lags and rate-to-FX feedback in the same parameter table. Declare any exclusion of a channel and the direction in which it could change results. Mark outputs as conditional simulations, accounting identities or break-even thresholds. Show sampling uncertainty separately if a parameter was estimated.

The sales and funding rules must operate in both worlds. Specify reserve constraints, how liquidity is supplied at the chosen policy rate, and the response when a floor binds. For a reproducible reference rate-to-FX link, use one-year interest parity in deviations. The rate contribution to 100 log(GEL/USD), alternative minus observed, is minus the sum of twelve expected monthly policy-rate differences divided by twelve (rates in annual percentage points). A persistent 1 pp increase over those twelve months thus implies a 1 log-point appreciation at the decision date, conditional on the same foreign rate, terminal currency value and risk premium for this channel. Use the rule's expected future rates under each policy. The direct FX purchase response carries the portfolio risk premium separately. This is an expectations assumption in a shallow market. Vary the horizon and test zero rate feedback, keeping each case's treatment distinct. Compare the resulting inflation response to a 100 bp rate impulse with the Georgian evidence as a diagnostic. [Georgian monetary transmission, Figure 11](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2024/english/1geoea2024002.pdf) Compare unit impulses and trajectories with available pre-2025 Georgian evidence and published model responses. Document differences in frequency, scale, shock definition and market regime. Treat a failure to discriminate among elasticities as information about uncertainty. A claim of an empirically identified GEL/USD effect would additionally require defensible variation independent of contemporaneous appreciation pressure. That separate claim has no bearing on whether a conditional simulation can be reported.

## 4. Economic cost and benefit accounts

Maintain three connected accounts: central-bank financial income/cost; the consolidated public-sector account; and domestic economic welfare. A payment between domestic residents can be a transfer rather than a net national resource loss. Exchange-rate valuation of public or private assets is not automatically a welfare cost. State the numeraire, price base, horizon and discounting before aggregation.

Measured reserve interest, fees and operation expenses are inputs. Incremental reserve financing may take the form of additional remunerated liabilities, lower outstanding refinancing assets and foregone lending income, or changes in nonremunerated base money. Use currency- and duration-matched average balances and rates. Policy rate multiplied by the reserve stock is not a measured financing cost. Domestic opportunity costs require a feasible alternative use of the resources and its risk-adjusted return.

Benefits to evaluate are the marginal reduction in external liquidity shortfalls and crisis losses, market-disruption risk and external financing costs attributable to the additional usable reserves. Deduct encumbrances and predetermined drains; do not assume all gross reserves can be spent. Calibrate private-bank FX buffers separately and test their accessibility in stress. ARA is a risk benchmark, not an estimated welfare optimum. An increase in ARA coverage cannot alone establish that the purchases were economically justified.

Consequences of the counterfactual exchange-rate path can include changes in external purchasing capacity, export and import margins, currency-mismatched external balance sheets and income from abroad. Quantify only the channels for which exposures and behavioral responses can be defended. Do not sum importer gains and exporter losses into national welfare without an internally consistent valuation and incidence framework. This is not a separate household-credit or general-inflation study.

Prevent double counting between crisis insurance, lower sovereign spreads and an FX liquidity buffer. A spread decrease is not a benefit caused by reserves until competing fiscal, global-rate and country-risk explanations have been assessed. The absence of a crisis does not measure insurance benefits.

## 5. Cangul and Abel benchmark

Use the authors' specific version, parameters, data vintages, normalization, objective, constraints, simulation horizon and replication materials. Preserve the existing algebraic audit as a limited supporting result. The printed derivative concern is not proof of an error in unpublished code; the isolated loss-channel calculation is not a replication of total utility or of the recommended reserve target.

Evaluate the benchmark's crisis probability, loss severity, reserve-return assumptions, sovereign-spread mechanism, dollarization mechanism and FX-buffer overlap against the episode data. Exact replication is required to claim reproduction of their optimum. An independent Georgian scenario or break-even calculation can proceed against the published benchmark at declared definitions and vintages. Failure to reproduce must be reported as an unresolved replication gap, not as proof that the recommendation is false. Benefits and costs must refer to the same horizon and resource constraint. The target level is relevant only insofar as it helps evaluate the extra reserves accumulated in the one episode.

## 6. Acceptance criteria for publication of conditional results

| Claim | Evidence required for that output |
|---|---|
| G1. Observed episode | Reconcile signed monthly flows to R1, mark gross lower bounds, residuals and vintage cutoffs. Show the sensitivity of purchase removal to hidden two-way trading. |
| G2. Conditional lari path | Use the named calibrations and common-shock experiment. Reproduce the observed baseline, recover zero differences under identical policies, check the unit impulse and demonstrate a feasible, finite path for each reported case. Show rate feedback, the no-direct-effect challenger and the persistence sensitivity. If the sign follows from the imposed kernel, say so. Report a sign-reversed diagnostic separately if reserve-confidence effects could offset the direct channel. |
| G3. Costs and incidence | Apply each accepted path to R4 funding alternatives and corrected R5 exposures. Report reserve yield, funding or foregone refinancing income, timing, unmatched consumption and transfer exposure. Label public financial costs and household partial effects separately. |
| G4. Reserve services | Reconcile stocks and flows with explicit residuals. Distinguish gross from usable assets, binding payments from sales, and cash coverage from avoided losses. Show the first binding call, financing alternative and break-even cost where welfare values are unknown. |
| G5. Joint answer | Match each cost and benefit to the same case, currency, dates, price basis, terminal convention and discounting. Cross FX and insurance cases, attribute changes one assumption at a time and report reversal thresholds. Sum only comparable, disjoint quantities. |

A scenario that violates accounting or cannot fund its assumed operations is reported with its first failing month, amount and reason. Withhold the affected path beyond that point and retain feasible cases. A failed central case leaves the central estimate unavailable. An aggregate welfare sign requires comparable resource valuations. Where valuations remain open, the release consists of the supported channels and the break-even condition. Scenario envelopes express variation across the named assumptions and carry no statistical coverage or probability. Earlier resampling thresholds, causal tests and full IMF replication govern only the claims for which they were designed.
